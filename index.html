How a company was counted as new

Every company that wins a federal contract registers with the government and gets an ID number. For each company above, the agency's contract records from Oct. 12, 2021 through Oct. 12, 2026 were searched for that ID. No record means it's listed here as new. As a second check, the company's exact name was searched too, and a match under a different ID is flagged "probably not new."

"Not competed" means the agency reported that the contract wasn't open to bids from other companies. There can be good reasons, like emergencies or only one supplier, but it's where reporters look first.

"Search donations" opens the Federal Election Commission's search for political donations from people who listed this company as their employer. Expect noise: small donations, old ones and other companies with similar names. Read each result before you treat it as a connection.

What this could get wrong

  • One company can have more than one ID, and a parent or subsidiary with a different name won't be caught.
  • Joint ventures get their own ID, so a "new" joint venture can be made of longtime contractors.
  • Only contracts count. Grants, loans, farm payments and subcontracts don't.
  • Many small purchases, like government credit card buys, aren't reported, so a company with small past sales may look new.
  • Agencies report late. Contracts from the last few days may be missing, and amounts change as contracts are modified.
  • Government records have errors, and they carry through to this list. Open each contract record before you rely on it.
